Calibrated Triage for Flow-Based Network Intrusion Detection on Real Traffic: A Hybrid Pre-Filter with Conformal Recall Control and a Controlled Comparison of Open-Set Escalation Gates
Ayush Verma1, Abhinay Chowdary1, Harsh Raj1
1Department of Computer Science and Engineering, SRM University AP. Guide: Mr. Nilin Prabhaker. Preprint draft, not peer reviewed.
Abstract— A real-time intrusion detector must pass benign traffic cheaply, catch known attacks, escalate unfamiliar traffic to analysts at a controlled rate, and explain what it escalates. We built and evaluated such a cascade on real traffic (CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and the corrected LYCOS-IDS2017) and report what held up. (i) The data needed cleaning first: 31% of the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 subsample were exact duplicate feature rows and 26% of chronological test rows also appeared in training. (ii) An unsupervised Isolation Forest pre-filter, common in the literature, was at chance on real data (attack-versus-benign AUROC 0.51). A hybrid pre-filter that takes the maximum of a supervised score and a nearest-benign-neighbour distance, each turned into a percentile against held-out benign traffic, reached 0.975 AUROC on known families and 0.835 on families withheld from training, and 0.999 / 0.992 on LYCOS-IDS2017 under a published zero-day protocol. (iii) Conformal recall control bounds the pre-filter's miss rate on known families; on a chronological stream with new families a static threshold missed 25.5% of attacks at a 5% budget, while adaptive conformal inference with analyst feedback met the budget (4.8%), and a detect-then-recalibrate baseline was more tolerant of feedback delay. (iv) Among six escalation gates, a k-nearest-neighbour distance escalated about twice as many flows from withheld families as OpenMax, energy or max-logit at the same cost (0.64 versus 0.32), and OpenMax was not distinguishable from softmax confidence (clustered p=0.63). (v) Retrieval grounding improved an LLM's ATT&CK technique attribution over an ungrounded model that was allowed to guess (84% versus 55%, McNemar p=1.1×10−5). We report negative results alongside positive ones, and every number traces to a run manifest.
Index Terms— network intrusion detection, conformal prediction, open-set recognition, concept drift, dataset quality, retrieval-augmented generation

1Introduction

Flow-based machine-learning detectors are usually evaluated as closed-set classifiers on public benchmarks, where they reach near-perfect accuracy. Deployed, they face three problems at once: almost all traffic is benign, so classifying every flow is wasteful; new attack families appear that were never in training; and analysts can review only a fixed number of alerts. A natural design is a cascade: a cheap pre-filter (Stage 1) passes benign traffic, a classifier (Stage 2) names known attacks, and an open-set gate escalates unfamiliar flows to an analyst at a calibrated rate, with an explanation.

We built such a cascade end to end (packet capture, Kafka streaming, scoring service, dashboard, LLM reasoner) and tested it on real traffic rather than on the synthetic fixture used during development. Several design choices that looked sound on the fixture did not survive. This paper reports both what failed and what replaced it.

Contributions.

  • A data audit of the CSE-CIC-IDS2018 subsample showing large duplicate and train/test overlap rates (Section 3).
  • A hybrid Stage 1 pre-filter (supervised score and nearest-benign-neighbour distance, fused by maximum over benign percentiles), selected by leave-one-family-out validation and shown to filter real traffic where an Isolation Forest does not (Section 5.1).
  • Conformal recall control for the pre-filter in three forms (pooled, per-family with a benign-cost cap, and adaptive with feedback), with a diagnosis of when each fails (Sections 5.1–5.2).
  • A controlled comparison of six open-set escalation gates under three protocols, including end to end through the cascade at matched analyst cost (Section 5.4).
  • A fairer test of retrieval grounding for LLM-based triage, which also exposed and fixed a retrieval-distractor failure (Section 5.7).

2Related Work

Open-set intrusion detection. OpenMax [1] recalibrates deep-network activations with Weibull tails to estimate the probability of an unknown class. VAEMax [15] combines OpenMax with per-class variational autoencoders on CIC-IDS2017 and CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and reports about 42% and 66% unknown-attack recall at under 5% benign false positives. An energy-based flow classifier [16] reaches AUROC above 0.99 on some held-out CICIDS2017 classes. CLAD/CLOSR [17] model benign traffic with a contrastive loss on LYCOS-IDS2017, with web SQL injection and Heartbleed held out as zero-days. We compare gates under VAEMax's and CLOSR's protocols, not only our own.

Conformal methods for intrusion detection. Split-conformal prediction [2], [3] gives finite-sample guarantees under exchangeability, and adaptive conformal inference [4] keeps long-run coverage under shift. CALIBURN [13] turns an alert budget into a threshold with conformal risk control; FIRCE and FADES [14] use conformal evaluation signals to detect drift and retrain; a robust conformal method [18] calibrates on attacker-perturbed traffic. We apply the conformal machinery to both sides of a cascade: the pre-filter's miss rate and the gate's escalation rate.

Evaluation and data quality. Stream assembly can reverse detector rankings on CICIDS2017 [19]; labelling and feature errors in CIC-IDS2017 and CSE-CIC-IDS2018 are documented in [20]; LYCOS-IDS2017 [21] re-extracts and relabels CIC-IDS2017. A Suricata and random-forest hybrid [22] found 0.97 benchmark F1 but 0.0095 recall on a laboratory capture. These results motivated our audit and our use of a corrected dataset.

3Data and its Problems

We use eight days of CSE-CIC-IDS2018 [7] processed flow features, subsampled to at most 20,000 rows per label and day (333,648 rows), and LYCOS-IDS2017 [21] produced by its authors' labelling script (1,837,498 flows). Both are mapped to 35 canonical flow features. Before modelling we audited for exact duplicate feature vectors.

4System

4.1 Cascade

Stage 1 scores every flow and passes those below a threshold as benign. Stage 2 is an XGBoost [11] classifier over known families. An escalation gate gives each Stage 1-flagged flow a novelty score; flows above a calibrated threshold go to an analyst with TreeSHAP [10] attributions, and a Tier-2 reasoner retrieves MITRE ATT&CK [8] context for an LLM [9]. Services exchange flows and alerts over Kafka.

4.2 Hybrid Stage 1

Two cheap scores are computed per flow: ssup, the attack probability from a 100-tree depth-6 XGBoost trained on benign versus attack flows, and sknn, the distance to the 10th nearest of up to 20,000 benign reference flows after a signed log transform and standardisation. Each is converted to a percentile against a held-out benign reference sample (20% of training benign flows, excluded from both fits), and the Stage 1 score is the larger:

score(x) = max( Fsup(ssup(x)), Fknn(sknn(x)) )

A flow is flagged if either view finds it unusual relative to benign traffic. A weighted average is available as an alternative.

4.3 Recall control for Stage 1

Pooled. With n calibration attack scores a(1) ≤ … ≤ a(n) and k = ⌊β(n+1)⌋, the threshold a(k) bounds the probability that an exchangeable new attack is passed as benign by k/(n+1) ≤ β. Per-family. Each family gets its own threshold and the lowest is used; under a cap on the share of calibration benign flows flagged, families whose threshold would breach it are waived and reported. Adaptive. Following [4], the miss level is updated after each analyst-confirmed attack, αt+1 = αt + γ(β − errt), bounding the long-run miss rate on attacks that receive feedback without exchangeability.

4.4 Escalation gates

We compare softmax confidence, OpenMax [1] on XGBoost margins, max-logit, energy, Mahalanobis distance and kNN distance on input features. Each threshold is a split-conformal quantile of the gate's scores at a 10% escalation budget; an adaptive version follows [4]. A deployed gate only sees flows Stage 1 flagged, so it is calibrated on those.

5Results

5.1 Stage 1 on real traffic

The Isolation Forest pre-filter had attack-versus-benign AUROC of 0.51 on the chronological split and 0.40 on a random split, and flagged 91–100% of benign flows at any useful miss budget. Replacements were chosen by leave-one-family-out (LOFO) validation, never on a test split.

With pooled recall control at a 5% miss budget on known families (random split), the hybrid met the budget (4.8% misses) while flagging 21% of benign flows on CSE-CIC-IDS2018 and 0.3% on LYCOS-IDS2017, against 99.9% for the Isolation Forest. It costs about 9 ms per flow and 9,400 flows/s in batches of 128, similar to the Isolation Forest.

5.2 New families over time

On the chronological CSE-CIC-IDS2018 split the test families (Botnet, Infiltration) are new. Pooled calibration flagged 92% of benign flows. The cause is not benign drift (median benign score 0.63 on validation, 0.64 on test) but the calibration attacks, which are 97% Infiltration: 29% of Infiltration flows have feature vectors identical to benign flows, and even a model trained on Infiltration reaches only 0.88 AUROC against benign, consistent with documented labelling problems in this dataset [20].

Stream A fits on the first 70% of time, calibrates on the next 15% and tests on the last 15%; stream B fits on 30%, calibrates on 15% and tests on 55%. The per-family calibration made the trade-off explicit by waiving Infiltration. Adaptive control met the budget on new families with prompt feedback; with a 1,000-flow delay a detect-then-recalibrate baseline in the style of [14] was more robust. Nothing held at a 10,000-flow delay (17–22% misses). The max-fusion hybrid should be paired with feedback: with a one-time calibration on known families it missed more new-family attacks than the weighted hybrid (29% versus 19%).

5.3 Evasion

On the random split, where Stage 1 filters about 80% of benign traffic, padding and slowing attack flows kept misses at 2.3–5.9% against a 5% budget; they often made attacks easier to catch. Moving every attacker-controllable feature 90% of the way to a real benign flow raised misses to 7.3% (10.8% with traffic-aware calibration [18]). Traffic-aware calibration lowered the benign cost from 20% to 15% and met the budget on average over its assumed perturbation mix (4.98%), but not for each perturbation. These are feature-space perturbations; gradient- and query-based attackers were not tested.

Stage 1 passed 71% of withheld-family flows on average (100% of Botnet and Brute Force, 77% of DoS/DDoS and Web Attack, 2% of Infiltration), which caps every gate. On Stage 2 alone (LOFO, 5 families × 5 seeds), unknown recall at a matched 10% false-escalation rate was kNN 0.58, Mahalanobis 0.42, softmax 0.20 and OpenMax 0.18. Testing significance with seeds averaged within each withheld family (the families, not the seeds, are the independent units), OpenMax and softmax did not differ (p=0.63 for recall and AUROC). Under the VAEMax protocol (fixed known/unknown classes, 5% benign false-positive budget) unknown recall was softmax 0.64, OpenMax 0.62 and kNN 0.24, although kNN had the best known-attack accuracy (0.89 versus 0.66). Adapting OpenMax to tree margins is therefore not a useful contribution on these data; the deployed system uses the kNN gate.

5.5 Escalation budget under drift

Calibrated on early days and evaluated on later ones (10 seeds, 10% budget), the static gate escalated 22–33% of known flows on the cleaned data (post-drift budget error 0.12–0.13), a KS detect-then-recalibrate gate reached an error of 0.015–0.017, and the adaptive gate 0.0009. On the uncleaned data the static gate failed in the opposite direction (near-zero escalation), so the direction of static failure depends on the data, while the adaptive gate held about 10.1% in both.

5.6 LYCOS-IDS2017, CLOSR-style protocol

With a 50/50 per-class split and SQL injection and Heartbleed only in test (3 seeds), benign-versus-attack AUROC was: depth-6 XGBoost 1.000 known / 0.990 zero-day (FPR at 95% TPR 0.015); max-fusion hybrid 0.999 / 0.992 (0.017); kNN alone 0.822 / 0.934; Isolation Forest 0.858 / 0.874. Here the zero-days resemble known families, so the supervised view dominates; max fusion keeps that strength while retaining the density view's advantage on entirely new families (Table II). CLOSR reports FPR at 95% TPR of 0.0073 and 0.0033 on these classes with 72 features and per-model tuning; with 11 and 12 test flows our zero-day numbers are noisy.

5.7 Retrieval-grounded triage

Six categories × 20 paired synthetic alerts, model gemini-3.1-flash-lite, no failed calls: grounded 101/120 (84%, 95% CI 77–90%), ungrounded but allowed to guess 66/120 (55%, CI 46–64%), ungrounded and told not to guess 0/120. McNemar exact: 49 versus 14 discordant pairs, p=1.1×10−5. An earlier pilot (9/9 versus 0/9) used only the last baseline, which overstates the effect. Grounded answers were correct in every category except Web Attack (1/20): retrieval ranked the right technique first but also passed a weak distractor (score 0.08 versus 0.24), which the model chose. Dropping retrieved techniques below half the top score fixed this on fresh trials (31/31 grounded versus 17/31, p=1.2×10−4; the run stopped at the daily API quota). Because the knowledge base also defines the correct answer, this measures use of retrieved context, not general ATT&CK knowledge.

5.8 Deployment checks

A live-capture run in containers produced 184–186 alerts end to end from sniffed packets and exposed a silent capture failure (missing libpcap) that replay tests could not. In a small case study on generated loopback traffic (ordinary requests, a request burst, connection probes), Suricata with 53,050 Emerging Threats rules raised no alerts; our cascade, calibrated on CSE-CIC-IDS2018, escalated every flow, and after calibration on a benign capture from the same host it held false alarms at 5% but flagged none of the burst or probe flows. Per-flow features do not see behaviour spread over many short flows, and thresholds do not transfer between networks without site calibration.

6Limitations

  • Results come from two public datasets with documented label problems; Infiltration in CSE-CIC-IDS2018 is close to inseparable from benign traffic.
  • Recall guarantees hold for known families under exchangeability. For new families only the adaptive control bounds misses; it needs confirmed labels and degrades with feedback delay.
  • Withheld families in LOFO and the two zero-day classes in LYCOS are few and small; several numbers rest on 2–3 seeds.
  • Evasion was tested with feature-space perturbations only.
  • The LLM study uses synthetic alerts, one small model and a knowledge base that also defines the answer.
  • The Suricata comparison is one short capture of generated traffic, not a benchmark.

7Conclusion

On real traffic, the assumptions that made the original cascade look strong on a synthetic fixture did not hold: the data needed cleaning, the unsupervised pre-filter did not filter, and the open-set head did not beat simple baselines. A hybrid pre-filter with conformal recall control makes the cascade's cost saving real on known families; adaptive control with analyst feedback extends the miss bound to new families; a kNN gate escalates twice as many unfamiliar flows as OpenMax at the same cost; and retrieval grounding measurably improves LLM triage once weak retrieved matches are removed. Code, run manifests and result files are in the project repository.
